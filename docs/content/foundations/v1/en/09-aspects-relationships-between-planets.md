# Aspects: Relationships Between Planets

## Orientation

An aspect describes an angular relationship between chart bodies or points. It is one of the ways a chart says that two factors are being considered together. The angle is a geometric relationship in the chart; the language used to discuss its possible meaning belongs to astrological interpretation.

This lesson introduces the five Foundations major aspects, the beginner idea of orb, and the vocabulary of applying, exact, and separating phase. It does not turn an aspect into a physical force, a good-or-bad score, or a complete reading of either body.

## Core Concept

### Geometry first, interpretation second

Zodiac positions can be represented as longitudes around a 360° circle. To identify an aspect, compare two positions using the smaller angular separation. A conjunction is near 0° separation, an opposition is 180°, a square is 90°, a trine is 120°, and a sextile is 60°. These degree relationships are the chart-geometry lane.

An astrologer may then use a tradition's vocabulary to explore the relationship. That second step is not another measurement. “The result lists a square between Mars and Saturn” is a chart statement if the result actually returns it. “A reader might explore pressure, effort, or a need for action between the two functions” is interpretive commentary. The commentary should name the bodies and preserve the context.

### The five major aspects

| Aspect | Target angle | Introductory interpretive vocabulary |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Conjunction** | 0° | Concentration, combination, or close merging of the factors under discussion. |
| **Opposition** | 180° | Polarity, tension, awareness, or a relationship that calls for perspective. |
| **Square** | 90° | Friction, pressure, challenge, or action between the factors. |
| **Trine** | 120° | Ease, flow, or a readily available connection. |
| **Sextile** | 60° | Opportunity, cooperation, or a connection that can be developed. |

These are conventional interpretive words, not universal formulas. A conjunction is not inherently positive; the bodies and context matter. An opposition is not automatically bad. A square can describe pressure that prompts useful work rather than a guaranteed conflict. A trine can feel easy without guaranteeing talent or success, and a sextile can indicate possibility without promising that anyone will use it.

### Orb: exact angle versus allowed tolerance

An **exact aspect** is at its target angle: for example, 90° for a square. A chart may still list an aspect when the separation is close but not exact. The allowed angular tolerance is its **orb**. If two bodies are 92° apart and a result calls that a square with a 2° orb, the geometric separation is 92°, the target is 90°, and the difference from exact is 2°.

Orb is a controlled chart term, not permission to invent a universal table. Different astrological practices can use different tolerances for different bodies or aspects. This lesson introduces the distinction so a learner can read a returned value; it does not disclose or prescribe an implementation-specific orb rule.

### Applying, exact, and separating

The Foundations vocabulary also includes three phase labels when the chart result provides them:

- **Applying** means the relevant relationship is conventionally described as moving toward exactness under the method used.
- **Exact** means the relationship is at the target angle for the relevant result or notation.
- **Separating** means it is conventionally described as having passed exactness and moving away from it under the method used.

These labels depend on motion and on the conventions used to determine the phase. They are not required to understand the five geometric types, and a learner should not reconstruct them from a static diagram. Read the returned label, keep it distinct from the aspect name, and do not turn it into a prediction.

### A relationship is not the whole story

The same aspect type can be discussed differently depending on the bodies or points involved. A conjunction between the Sun and Moon raises different symbolic questions from a conjunction between Mercury and Venus. A square involving Mars is not automatically the same kind of story as a square involving two slower bodies. The bodies, their signs and houses when supported, the orb, phase, broader chart priorities, and the chosen interpretive approach all matter.

Nor does an aspect operate as a physical push or pull. The positions and angular relationship are chart facts produced by the calculation boundary; the meaning is a convention used by astrologers. NatalNode keeps the statement simple: **Calculated with astronomy. Interpreted through astrology.** Precision in the first lane does not scientifically validate the second.

### Keep the scope Foundations-sized

This lesson does not expand into minor aspects, harmonics, declination parallels, midpoints, aspect-pattern doctrine, complex orb weighting, or quantitative aspect scoring. It also does not teach a universal rule for “unaspected” bodies. Those topics may be treated differently by different schools and are outside this first introduction.

## Professional Vocabulary

- **Aspect** — an angular relationship between chart bodies or points that may be interpreted through an astrological approach.
- **Conjunction** — a major aspect with a target angle of 0°.
- **Opposition** — a major aspect with a target angle of 180°.
- **Square** — a major aspect with a target angle of 90°.
- **Trine** — a major aspect with a target angle of 120°.
- **Sextile** — a major aspect with a target angle of 60°.
- **Orb** — the angular tolerance between an observed separation and an aspect's target angle.
- **Applying** — a returned phase label for a relationship conventionally moving toward exactness under the relevant method.
- **Exact** — a phase or notation label indicating the target relationship is exact for the relevant result.
- **Separating** — a returned phase label for a relationship conventionally moving away from exactness under the relevant method.
- **Angular separation** — the smaller angle between two positions on a circle.

## Example

The following synthetic positions are made for a geometry exercise. They are not a real person's chart and do not assert an interpretation.

| Body A | Zodiac longitude | Body B | Zodiac longitude | Smallest separation | Geometry |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Sun at 20° Taurus | 50° | Mercury at 22° Taurus | 52° | 2° | Conjunction within a 2° orb |
| Sun at 10° Aries | 10° | Moon at 10° Libra | 190° | 180° | Exact opposition |
| Mars at 15° Cancer | 105° | Saturn at 15° Libra | 195° | 90° | Exact square |
| Venus at 12° Leo | 132° | Jupiter at 12° Sagittarius | 252° | 120° | Exact trine |
| Mercury at 5° Gemini | 65° | Venus at 5° Leo | 125° | 60° | Exact sextile |

Using the zodiac order (Aries 0°, Taurus 30°, Gemini 60°, Cancer 90°, Leo 120°, Libra 180°, Sagittarius 240°), the first pair is 2° from the 0° conjunction target; the remaining pairs match their targets exactly.

Interpretation remains careful: the Sun–Moon opposition might invite questions about polarity or awareness, while the Mars–Saturn square might invite questions about pressure or directed effort. Neither causes conflict, guarantees a gift, or decides character; each reading needs the bodies and wider chart context.

## My Chart

Open **My Chart** when a result is available, and inspect returned aspect entries rather than calculating from a picture.

- **Chart exists:** identify the bodies or points, aspect type, orb, and phase labels that are actually returned. Separate “the result lists…” from “one conventional reading might…”. If an aspect is not returned, do not add it because the geometry looks suggestive.
- **Chart exists with unknown birth time:** preserve the unknown status and follow the fields the result supports. If an aspect is returned, describe it as a returned chart fact; if the result suppresses or limits time-sensitive data, do not recreate the aspect from an assumed time.
- **No chart exists:** use the synthetic table or the normal chart-entry boundary. The lesson remains readable without a personal chart.

The My Chart path is an observation boundary, not a browser-side astronomy or aspect calculator.

## Practice

Use the synthetic table in the Example section.

1. Verify the longitude and smallest separation for the Sun at 10° Aries and Moon at 10° Libra.
2. Which row is within an orb rather than exact at the target angle?
3. Match each aspect with one cautious vocabulary pair: conjunction with concentration/combination; opposition with polarity/awareness; square with friction/action; trine with ease/flow; sextile with opportunity/cooperation.
4. Rewrite “A square is bad” so that it preserves the geometric fact and makes the interpretation conditional.
5. Explain why the same trine vocabulary cannot be interpreted without knowing which bodies are involved.
6. If a result reports “applying,” “exact,” or “separating,” copy the label and explain that it is a returned phase state, not a forecast.

Suggested answers: Aries–Libra are 10° and 190°, exactly 180° apart; Sun–Mercury is 2° from its target. Name the bodies, aspect, context, and limits. A square may be explored as pressure or action in a stated convention, but is not inherently bad or causal.

## Check Understanding

### Question 1

Which statement keeps aspect geometry and interpretation in separate lanes?

- A. A square is a physical force that causes conflict.
- B. An aspect is an angular relationship in the chart; an astrologer may then interpret it through a convention that also considers the bodies and wider context.
- C. The aspect name is enough, even when the bodies are unknown.
- D. A trine scientifically proves talent.

**Correct answer:** B.

**Explanation:** The angular separation is a chart-geometry fact. Meaning is interpretive and depends on the bodies, method, context, and limits; the aspect name alone is not a complete reading.

### Question 2

What does an orb describe?

- A. A guaranteed strength score for an aspect.
- B. The difference between an observed angular separation and the aspect's target angle, within an allowed tolerance.
- C. The house in which a planet must be located.
- D. A replacement for naming the two bodies involved.

**Correct answer:** B.

**Explanation:** Orb distinguishes exact angle from a permitted angular tolerance. It helps describe why a near-angle relationship may be listed, but it is not a universal score or a complete interpretation.

## Key Takeaway

Aspects are angular relationships whose five Foundations types target 0°, 180°, 90°, 120°, and 60°. Orb and phase labels refine how a returned relationship is described, while interpretation remains a qualified convention shaped by the bodies and wider chart context—not a good/bad verdict or physical cause.
