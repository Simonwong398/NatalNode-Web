# Foundations V1 Final Assessment

This is the canonical-English final check for Foundations V1. It assesses the learner's ability to keep chart facts, astrological interpretation, uncertainty, and responsible use distinct.

The check contains exactly ten questions. A result of 8/10 is a pass, and retries are unlimited. Passing is not certification and does not replace completion of the twelve lessons. The final check is not required for Foundations Core readiness; a future reward system may use a passing result as an eligibility input, but no reward behavior is defined here.

This document is assessment content only. It does not define assessment UI, runtime scoring, persistence, account state, entitlement, reward issuance, or a server boundary.

## Question 01 — Chart fact and interpretation

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q01.chart_fact_interpretation`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.natal_chart`

A returned chart record states: “Sun: 14° Taurus.” Which statement is a chart fact rather than an interpretation?

**A.** This placement guarantees that the person will prefer stable work.

**B.** This person is permanently cautious because Taurus is an earth sign.

**C.** The returned result lists the Sun at 14° Taurus.

**D.** The placement proves that the person's identity is fixed.

**Correct answer:** **C**

**Rationale:** The returned placement is a reported chart value. Statements about preference, identity, or guarantees add an interpretive claim and should remain qualified.

## Question 02 — Sign and astronomical constellation

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q02.sign_constellation_distinction`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.zodiac_structure`

Which statement best distinguishes an astrological sign from an astronomical constellation?

**A.** A sign is a physical object in space, while a constellation is a personality category.

**B.** A sign is a 30-degree sector in an astrological zodiac framework, while a constellation is an astronomical sky region with official boundaries; names can overlap without their boundaries or purposes being the same.

**C.** The twelve signs and the 88 constellations are identical divisions of equal size.

**D.** A constellation is only a different name for a house in a natal chart.

**Correct answer:** **B**

**Rationale:** Foundations uses signs as a twelve-part astrological coordinate vocabulary. Astronomical constellations are officially bounded sky regions of unequal size, so shared names do not make the systems interchangeable.

## Question 03 — Unknown birth time

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q03.unknown_birth_time`

**Linked lessons:** `foundations.chart_calculation`, `foundations.houses_angles`, `foundations.whole_chart`

A person knows the birth date and place but does not know the exact birth time. What is the responsible first step?

**A.** Preserve the unknown time and omit or qualify the Ascendant, Midheaven, houses, cusps, and other time-dependent claims.

**B.** Use noon because it is the least biased substitute.

**C.** Use midnight and present the resulting angles as exact.

**D.** Infer the time from the person's personality so that every chart layer is complete.

**Correct answer:** **A**

**Rationale:** Unknown time is valid input, not permission to insert a convenient time. The missing input limits what can be calculated and interpreted, and the limitation must remain visible.

## Question 04 — Local civil time, time zones, and historical rules

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q04.local_time_and_timezone`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.chart_calculation`

Which handling of a recorded birth time is consistent with the Foundations calculation boundary?

**A.** Treat the clock reading as UTC regardless of the birthplace.

**B.** Use the current offset for the city even when the birth occurred under different historical rules.

**C.** Choose whichever offset produces the clearest interpretation.

**D.** Start with the original local civil date, time, and place; apply the applicable historical time-zone and daylight-saving rules to identify the instant, while keeping ambiguous or nonexistent readings visible.

**Correct answer:** **D**

**Rationale:** The original local civil record and location are the starting inputs. Historical time rules can change the applicable offset, and ambiguity must be reported rather than silently resolved for convenience.

## Question 05 — Planet × Sign × House

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q05.planet_sign_house_synthesis`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.planet_sign_house`

A supported chart result places Mars in Virgo in the 10th House. Which is the best bounded first interpretation?

**A.** Mars in Virgo in the 10th House proves the person will become a demanding manager.

**B.** Keep the layers distinct: explore Mars as the function of pursuit or action, Virgo as a possible style of refinement or practicality, and the 10th House as a supported public-role context within a named approach.

**C.** Ignore Mars and read Virgo as the person's complete personality.

**D.** Treat the 10th House as evidence that a specific career outcome is inevitable.

**Correct answer:** **B**

**Rationale:** The three layers answer different questions and can be combined only as a qualified symbolic reading. None of them establishes a fixed trait or guaranteed outcome by itself.

## Question 06 — Aspect geometry

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q06.major_aspect_geometry`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.aspects`

A result lists the Sun at 10° and the Moon at 100° on the same zodiac circle. Their shortest angular separation is 90°. Which major aspect name matches that geometry?

**A.** Conjunction

**B.** Opposition

**C.** Square

**D.** Trine

**Correct answer:** **C**

**Rationale:** A 90° separation is the target geometry for a square. The aspect name reports an angular relationship; it does not by itself determine whether an interpretation is positive, negative, or predictive.

## Question 07 — Houses, angles, and cusps

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q07.houses_angles_cusps`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.houses_angles`

Which pairing uses the Foundations vocabulary correctly?

**A.** The Ascendant and Midheaven are time-and-place-sensitive reference points; houses are chart-space sectors; a house cusp is a boundary or starting point for a house.

**B.** Signs and houses are interchangeable names for the same twelve divisions.

**C.** The Midheaven is the astronomical zenith, and the Ascendant is a house cusp only.

**D.** An empty house is not part of the chart, so it has no section or cusp.

**Correct answer:** **A**

**Rationale:** Angles, houses, and cusps are related but distinct chart layers. Their time-and-place sensitivity also means that unsupported birth-time data cannot justify precise house or angle claims.

## Question 08 — Prioritization and whole-chart synthesis

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q08.prioritization_and_synthesis`

**Linked lessons:** `foundations.chart_priorities`, `foundations.whole_chart`

Which reading plan follows the Foundations method?

**A.** Assign numerical strength scores to every planet and let the highest score explain the person.

**B.** Give the most dramatic keyword all the attention and discard factors that complicate it.

**C.** Treat one placement as a complete identity statement so that the whole chart has one simple answer.

**D.** Check supported inputs and chart structure first, then consider relevant or repeated relationships, connect only supported factors, qualify tensions, and stop when the evidence runs out; the whole chart is not the whole person.

**Correct answer:** **D**

**Rationale:** Prioritization is a transparent order for explanation, not a dominance score. Whole-chart synthesis connects a small number of supported threads while keeping uncertainty, competing themes, and the boundary around the whole person visible.

## Question 09 — Astronomical precision and scientific validation

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q09.precision_is_not_validation`

**Linked lessons:** `foundations.chart_calculation`, `foundations.responsible_astrology`

A calculation uses the correct local time, historical time-zone rules, coordinates, and astronomical positions, and reports an aspect precisely. What conclusion follows?

**A.** The precision proves that the symbolic interpretation causes events.

**B.** The more precise the chart, the more scientifically validated every interpretation becomes.

**C.** Precision can improve the faithfulness of the returned astronomical and chart geometry, but it does not scientifically validate symbolic, causal, personality, or predictive claims.

**D.** Once the geometry is precise, uncertainty and method no longer need to be stated.

**Correct answer:** **C**

**Rationale:** Calculation and interpretation use different evidence lanes. Accurate input handling can support an accurate chart result without turning an astrological convention into scientific validation.

## Question 10 — Responsible use and high-stakes claims

**Question ID:** `foundations.final_check.q10.responsible_use`

**Linked lesson:** `foundations.responsible_astrology`

A user asks astrology to diagnose depression and decide whether they should stop prescribed medication. What is the responsible response?

**A.** Provide a confident diagnosis if several placements seem to agree.

**B.** Decline the diagnosis and high-stakes decision authority, explain that astrology is not a clinical or medical assessment, encourage qualified care, and protect the person's consent and privacy; offer only a narrower reflective question if appropriate.

**C.** Ask for a third party's birth data and compare charts before answering.

**D.** Present the chart as a warning but state that the user remains legally responsible for the decision.

**Correct answer:** **B**

**Rationale:** Responsible astrology refuses diagnosis and high-stakes certainty. It keeps agency and privacy intact and points toward qualified support when the consequence of being wrong is serious.
