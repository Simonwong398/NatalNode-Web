# Planet × Sign × House

## Orientation

Lessons 04–06 introduced chart bodies and their broad functions; Lesson 07 added houses as contexts. This lesson brings them together as a beginner interpretive scaffold: identify the body/function, notice sign style, then consider house context when supported.

The multiplication sign is not a scientific equation. It reminds us to keep three questions visible and to separate what the result reports, what a convention may suggest, and what remains unknown.

## Core Concept

### Keep the layers separate first

Use this working map:

| Layer | Beginner question | Safe contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Planet or body** | What function or subject is being discussed? | It identifies the focus, such as Sun orientation or Mercury's information exchange. |
| **Sign** | Through what style or vocabulary might it be described? | It qualifies how the function may be expressed in a chosen approach. |
| **House** | In what chart context might it be emphasized? | It adds a setting layer when the placement is supported. |

This “what / how / where” shorthand is useful at Foundations depth, but it is not a complete or universal formula. A body has several themes, a sign does not force a style, and a house does not predict an event. Aspects, priorities, tradition, input quality, and lived context can change the reading.

### A repeatable first-pass order

When a chart result supplies all three layers, use the following order:

1. **Identify the body and function.** Name its course vocabulary; the Sun, for example, can open a question about orientation, vitality, agency, or purpose.
2. **Identify the sign and style.** Use a small amount of conventional vocabulary without replacing the body.
3. **Identify the house and context.** Name the life-area context and selected system when relevant; do not turn “where” into an outcome.
4. **Combine cautiously.** Join the layers while showing their different jobs.
5. **Qualify the result.** Say which facts support the sentence and what it does not establish.

This is a writing aid, not a rule for every school. Its purpose is to stop one keyword from doing the work of a complete reading.

### Facts and interpretation use different lanes

“The returned chart lists the Sun in Aries in the 1st House” is a chart statement if those fields are present. “This may invite reflection on direct ways of presenting intentions” is interpretation, not a new calculated fact or a conclusion proved by the first sentence.

NatalNode's trust boundary remains: **Calculated with astronomy. Interpreted through astrology.** Calculation reports positions and supported assignments; interpretation uses a stated convention to discuss possible meaning. Do not write a convention as if it were a measurement.

### Examples that change one layer at a time

Consider three synthetic cards for learning, not personal charts or NatalNode calculations.

**Card A: same body, sign, and house**

- Sun in Aries, 1st House.

The body keeps the discussion on orientation, vitality, agency, or purpose. Aries can supply vocabulary such as initiative or beginning, while the 1st House supplies presence and immediate orientation. A bounded sentence might explore how agency is approached directly in settings involving presence or new beginnings. This is a prompt, not a claim that the person must be forceful or that an event will occur.

**Card B: same body and house, different sign**

- Sun in Libra, 1st House.

The Sun question and 1st-House context remain, but the sign vocabulary changes. A reader might explore balance, reciprocity, comparison, or negotiation when discussing orientation and presence. Changing the sign does not make Libra a personality guarantee.

**Card C: same body and sign, different house**

- Sun in Aries, 10th House.

The body and sign remain as in Card A, while the context shifts toward public role, vocation, visibility, responsibility, or contribution. A cautious sentence might ask how initiative and agency are considered in public contexts; it does not guarantee a career, status, or leadership role.

The comparison is the point: changing the sign changes the body's style vocabulary, while changing the house changes its context. Neither change produces one fixed sentence.

### Why this is not cookbook astrology

A cookbook approach assigns one stock meaning to every combination: “Sun in Aries in the 1st House equals X.” It hides the convention and turns a symbolic prompt into a verdict. The scaffold should make source facts auditable and the interpretation visibly provisional.

Avoid giant combination lists. Several bodies can share a house, one can have several aspects, and schools can weigh the same factor differently. A three-layer statement is still only part of a chart. Lesson 09 adds aspects; Lessons 10–11 add prioritization and whole-chart synthesis.

### When the house layer is unavailable

If reliable birth time is absent and the result does not support houses, stop at the supported layers. It may support “the Sun is in Aries” without supporting “the Sun is in the 1st House.” Discuss body and sign with a qualified convention, then state “house context unavailable for this result.”

Do not use noon, midnight, a rectification guess, a person's name, or the sign itself to fill the house layer. A missing house is not an invitation to complete the pattern.

## Professional Vocabulary

- **Planet / chart body** — the chart object that supplies the focus of a symbolic discussion.
- **Sign placement** — the zodiac-framework sector in which a body is reported.
- **House placement** — the chart-space sector in which a body is reported when the input and selected system support it.
- **Interpretive scaffold** — a limited teaching structure that helps organize a reading without claiming to be a complete theory.
- **Context** — the surrounding chart, method, input quality, and lived situation used to qualify an interpretation.
- **Bounded interpretation** — an interpretation that names its supporting facts, uses proportionate language, and preserves uncertainty.
- **Chart fact** — a value or relationship returned by the chart result, kept distinct from the sentence a reader builds from it.

## Example

Use this synthetic worksheet:

| Body | Sign | House | First-pass notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mercury | Virgo | 3rd House | Information processing; detail/refinement vocabulary; communication and everyday learning context. |
| Venus | Libra | 10th House | Valuation and relating; balance/reciprocity vocabulary; public role or visible contribution context. |
| Mars | Capricorn | 6th House | Directed action; structure/persistence vocabulary; routines, service, or maintenance context. |

Write one sentence for each row using this frame: “The chart reports [body] in [sign] in [house]. One conventional reading might explore [qualified connection], while the placement alone does not establish [overclaim].”

For Mercury, a safe connection might explore how attention to detail is brought into communication or learning routines. For Venus, it might explore how reciprocity is considered in visible contribution or public collaboration. For Mars, it might explore how structured effort is considered in daily practice. These are prompts, not diagnoses, rankings, or predictions. The table is deliberately small; it is not a replacement for the rest of the chart.

## My Chart

Open **My Chart** when an actual chart result is available, and inspect each layer in order.

- **Chart exists with a supported house layer:** copy the returned body, sign, house, and selected-system facts before writing a sentence. Mark the first part as chart information and the second part as interpretation.
- **Chart exists but birth time is unknown or houses are omitted:** use only supported body/sign facts. Say that house context is unavailable or limited; do not substitute a time or infer a house from the sign.
- **No chart exists:** use the normal chart-entry boundary if a personal exercise is desired. Otherwise use the synthetic worksheet. Do not invent a personal placement.

The My Chart path helps a learner inspect returned facts. It does not authorize the lesson, browser, or client to calculate a body, sign, or house.

## Practice

Use the three synthetic cards above.

1. Write the body/function sentence for the Sun before adding a sign or house.
2. For Card A, add two cautious Aries style words and one 1st-House context word.
3. Compare Cards A and B. Identify the layer that changed and the layer that stayed stable.
4. Compare Cards A and C. Identify the layer that changed and explain why the result is not a career guarantee.
5. Rewrite “Mars in the 6th House causes workplace conflict” so that it names a possible interpretive context and removes the causal claim.
6. Remove the house from a card and write the safe sentence a reader should use when birth time is unknown.

Suggested approach: keep body/function, sign/style, and house/context separate, then qualify the connection. For example, Mars in the 6th House can invite reflection on directed effort in daily work or maintenance; it does not cause a workplace event.

## Check Understanding

### Question 1

Which sequence best represents a bounded beginner synthesis?

- A. Start with a stereotype, choose a sign to justify it, and infer a house if one is missing.
- B. Identify the body/function, add sign/style, add supported house/context, combine cautiously, and qualify the interpretation.
- C. Treat the sign as the body and the house as a guaranteed event.
- D. Read one placement as the complete explanation of a person.

**Correct answer:** B.

**Explanation:** The sequence keeps the three layers distinct before combining them. It also requires supported inputs and qualified language rather than a stock result.

### Question 2

What should a learner do when a result supports a body's sign but does not support its house because birth time is unknown?

- A. Use the sign to determine the house.
- B. Choose the 1st House as a neutral default.
- C. Discuss the supported body/sign layers and state that the house context is unavailable or limited.
- D. Present a complete Planet × Sign × House sentence anyway.

**Correct answer:** C.

**Explanation:** A missing house is a real limitation, not a blank to fill. Preserving the unknown state keeps the interpretation tied to returned facts.

## Key Takeaway

Planet × Sign × House is a beginner scaffold: identify the body function, qualify it with sign style, add supported house context, and then write a cautious interpretation. It is not a complete formula, a cookbook, or a substitute for aspects, chart priorities, whole-chart context, and reliable input.
