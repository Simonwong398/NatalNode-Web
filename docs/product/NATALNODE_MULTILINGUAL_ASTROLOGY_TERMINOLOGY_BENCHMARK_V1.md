# NATALNODE MULTILINGUAL ASTROLOGY TERMINOLOGY BENCHMARK V1

**STATUS: RESEARCH BENCHMARK — NO PRODUCTION TERMS APPROVED**

- Task: WEB-LOCALIZATION-001L
- Evidence date: 2026-08-31
- Scope: English canonical baseline plus es, fr, de, ja, ko, and zh-Hant
- Required coverage: all 51 P0 concepts in NATALNODE_ASTROLOGY_GLOSSARY_V1
- P1 and P2 concepts: not benchmarked in this minimum slice

## Executive result

This document is a bounded terminology benchmark for later review. It records public-web observations, conservative locale candidates, adaptation modes, confidence, and unresolved questions. It is not a translation file, a production glossary update, or approval of any non-English term.

The P0 matrix covers 51 concepts for each of the six target locales. The English terms remain the canonical semantic baseline. Every non-English candidate in the matrix is explicitly marked PROPOSED_NOT_APPROVED. No semantic concept ID, glossary taxonomy, runtime source, or production locale variant is changed by this task.

Astrology terminology is not uniform across languages, regions, schools, or software products. A term appearing on a public site is evidence of usage, not evidence that the term is scientifically validated or that it should be the sole NatalNode production label. Where observations diverge, this benchmark preserves the disagreement and sends the item to review.

## Scope and method

### Included

- The 51 P0 concepts already defined by the canonical glossary.
- Four cross-locale benchmark surfaces: Astro.com/Astrodienst, Astro-Seek, Astrotheme where a French observation was applicable, and Cafe Astrology for English notation and aliases.
- Additional independent Japanese, Korean, and Traditional-Chinese sources where the primary benchmark surfaces did not expose enough localized labels.
- Candidate display terms, short forms, aliases, glyphs, Latin or historical aliases, and a locale adaptation mode for every matrix row.
- Separate adaptation profiles for Japanese and Korean, including raw-English display and bilingual-first-use recommendations.

### Excluded

- P1 concepts: sign elements, sign modalities, direct motion, stationary motion, and tropical zodiac.
- P2 concepts: ingress, station, lunation, eclipse, and full moon.
- Translation of explanatory articles, interpretations, UI copy, SEO content, or legal text.
- Any claim that a locale candidate is approved, current, production-ready, scientifically authoritative, or legally safe without owner review.
- Any automatic conversion from Simplified Chinese to Traditional Chinese.

### Source hierarchy

1. A first-party or established public astrology product page with a visible localized label.
2. A public glossary, chart, settings page, or educational page from an independent locale-specific source.
3. A public English source for canonical notation, glyphs, and historical aliases only.
4. Search-result snippets or an accessible page when the site challenged automated access. These are recorded as bounded observations and receive lower confidence when they are not corroborated.

The benchmark records the four primary site columns separately. A dash means that the bounded sample did not expose an exact usable label for that row; it does not mean the site never uses the concept. Astrotheme is not applicable outside the French matrix. Cafe Astrology is an English notation and alias baseline, not multilingual evidence.

The Astro.com, Astro-Seek, Astrotheme, and Cafe Astrology pages were inspected on 2026-08-31. Local-language observations were independently checked against the URLs listed in the source index. The Traditional-Chinese evidence was taken from pages served as zh-TW, Traditional-Chinese Hong Kong/Taiwan material, or Traditional-Chinese Wikipedia; Simplified-Chinese-only pages were excluded.

### Matrix field grammar

Each locale matrix has one row per semantic concept and a compact benchmark record. The record keys are the required fields:

AC = Astro.com observed term; AS = Astro-Seek observed term; AT = Astrotheme observed term if applicable; CA = Cafe Astrology relevant English notation or alias; local = additional local-language observations; candidate = recommended candidate; short = short form; aliases = alternate display aliases; glyph = glyph or notation; latin = Latin or historical alias; mode = LOCALE_ADAPTATION_MODE; bf = BILINGUAL_FIRST_USE; consensus = consensus classification; confidence = confidence; note = review note; refs = source references; status = publication status.

The table columns before the record are semanticConceptId, canonicalEnglish, family, and priority. Thus every row has the full required field set without copying the long field names into every cell. Empty values are intentional and shown as —.

## Locale Adaptation Mode taxonomy

| Mode | Use in this benchmark |
|---|---|
| NATIVE_TERM | Established local-language term is the proposed primary display label. |
| ESTABLISHED_LOANWORD | Local professional usage has adopted a loanword that is readable in the locale. |
| TRANSLITERATION_STANDARD | A proper or technical term is conventionally transliterated into the locale script. |
| INTERNATIONAL_ABBREVIATION | ASC, MC, Rx, or another cross-locale abbreviation is retained as a compact secondary label. |
| INTERNATIONAL_GLYPH | A conventional sign, planet, aspect, or chart glyph is retained. |
| LATIN_HISTORICAL_ALIAS | A Latin or historical form is recorded as an alias, not as the primary translation. |
| PROPER_NAME_RETAINED | A named house system, person, or technical proper name is retained. |
| BILINGUAL_FIRST_USE | Local candidate is paired with English or Latin at first use because the technical mapping remains review-sensitive. |
| ENGLISH_RETAINED | English is intentionally retained where the bounded local evidence is not enough for a safer proposal. |
| MIXED_PROFESSIONAL_USAGE | Public professional usage visibly mixes local language, loanword, abbreviation, or English. |
| INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE | The benchmark does not support a responsible locale candidate yet. |
| REVIEW_REQUIRED | A candidate can be shown for discussion but needs an explicit terminology decision before production. |

Modes are descriptive labels for the proposed display strategy. They are not approval states. A row can use BILINGUAL_FIRST_USE in the bf field while its primary mode remains a local term, loanword, or REVIEW_REQUIRED.

## Consensus and confidence

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| convergent | Two or more relevant public observations support the same usable concept label or an unambiguous family of forms. |
| moderate | Evidence is usable but comes from fewer surfaces, a mixed professional convention, or a small amount of inflectional variation. |
| split | Public usage contains materially different labels or a local term plus a strong English/Latin alternative. |
| insufficient | The bounded evidence is too thin or too inconsistent to recommend a production-facing primary label. |

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| high | Direct or independently corroborated observations support a conservative candidate. |
| medium | The concept is clear, but the preferred wording, register, or display pairing needs owner review. |
| low | Evidence is sparse, source access was constrained, or the candidate may be school- or region-specific. |

## Locale adaptation profiles

### Spanish (es)

Spanish Astro.com pages expose direct terms for the planets, signs, aspects, houses, Ascendente, Medio Cielo, orbe, and house-system labels. The conservative profile is native Spanish for descriptive concepts, with Placidus retained as a proper name and ASC and MC retained as international secondary notation. The main review risk is grammatical agreement for applying, exact, and separating aspects, plus the choice between a descriptive Whole Sign phrase and a short product label.

Recommended hierarchy:

1. Established Spanish descriptive term.
2. Established international proper name or glyph.
3. Spanish term plus ASC or MC where the abbreviation is useful.
4. Bilingual or review-only wording for technical phase states when a compact label would hide the grammatical choice.

Raw English should not be the primary display language for core P0 concepts in Spanish.

### French (fr)

French Astro.com and Astrotheme pages provide direct observations for the core aspect, angle, house, orb, and house-system vocabulary. The profile favors native French terms such as Ascendant, Milieu du Ciel, Carré, Trigone, and Orbe. Placidus remains a retained proper name. Astrotheme independently exposes appliquant and séparant language, but the exact inflection and noun agreement still require a product decision.

Raw English should not be the primary display language for core P0 concepts in French. MC, ASC, and Medium Coeli can remain secondary notation or aliases.

### German (de)

German Astro.com and Astrowiki observations support Sonne, Mond, the German sign names, Aszendent, Medium Coeli/Himmelsmitte, Häuser, Häuserspitze, Orbis, and the major aspect names. The profile favors native German descriptive labels, retains Placidus, and uses ASC and MC as international notation. Applikativ and separativ are observed technical forms but need inflection and style review.

Raw English should not be the primary display language for core P0 concepts in German.

### Japanese (ja)

Japanese terminology is intentionally not flattened into raw English. The observed hierarchy is:

1. Native kanji body and sign terms where established: 太陽, 月, 牡羊座, and related forms.
2. Established katakana loanwords used by Japanese astrology tools: アセンダント, コンジャンクション, オーブ, and similar terms.
3. Japanese term paired with English or Latin at first use for technical controls and system names.
4. Raw English only as a secondary fallback when the local technical mapping remains unresolved.

The recommended raw-English-primary-display policy is **NO** for core P0 concepts. English may appear secondarily for unresolved technical states, not as the first visible label.

The working matrix count of bilingual-first-use candidates is **8**: Ascendant, Midheaven, Placidus, Whole Sign, Equal House, applying, exact, and separating. This is a review queue count, not a production decision. Japanese sources strongly support native kanji for bodies and signs and established katakana for aspects and chart systems, but aspect-phase labels remain less convergent than the main aspect names.

Japanese profile observations:

- Bodies and signs: native kanji are primary and should not be replaced by raw English.
- Angles: アセンダント is strong; ミッドヘブン and メディウム・コエリ coexist for MC.
- Houses: 1ハウス and 1室 both occur; a consistent product convention is required.
- House systems: プラシーダス, ホールサイン, and イコールハウス are readable, but the first-use pairing should explain the English system name.
- Aspects: katakana names are widely observable; compact Japanese kanji aspect aliases are useful as aliases only until product register is decided.
- Phases: アプライング, イグザクト, and セパレーティング are observed or inferable in professional usage, but their display grammar and Japanese-first alternatives need review.

### Korean (ko)

Korean usage is more visibly mixed between translated Hangul, Sino-Korean technical terms, established loanwords, and English/Latin notation. The observed hierarchy is:

1. Convergent Hangul body and sign terms.
2. Established Korean technical label or translated term.
3. Korean plus English/Latin at first use for angles, house systems, orb, and aspect-phase controls.
4. Raw English selectively for technical controls where it is the established professional convention, never as the default for core bodies or signs.

The recommended raw-English-primary-display policy is **SELECTIVE YES** for technical controls only. It is **NO** for core bodies, signs, and the main user-facing chart labels.

The working matrix count of bilingual-first-use candidates is **9**: Ascendant, Midheaven, Placidus, Whole Sign, Equal House, orb, applying, exact, and separating. This count identifies terms where Korean plus English/Latin is safer for first exposure; it is not an approval count.

Korean profile observations:

- Bodies and signs: Hangul labels such as 태양, 달, 수성, and 양자리 are supported by Korean chart and educational sources.
- Angles: 상승점 and 중천점 are genuine Korean terms, while 어센던트 and 미드헤븐 remain common technical alternatives.
- Houses: 하우스 and 하우스 커스프 are common product terms; 제1하우스 and 1하우스 are both readable.
- House systems: 플라시두스, 홀사인 하우스, and 이퀄 하우스 occur in professional tools.
- Aspects: 합, 충, 사각, 삼각, and 육각 are supported alongside 컨정션, 오포지션, 스퀘어, 트라인, and 섹스타일.
- Phases and orb: 어플라잉, 익젝트, 세퍼레이팅, 오브, 수렴, and 발산 appear in mixed usage. This is the highest-risk Korean terminology group.

### Material Japanese versus Korean differences

Japanese has a clearer native-kanji layer for bodies and signs and a stable katakana layer for many technical astrology words. Korean also has strong Hangul body and sign terms, but professional tools more visibly alternate between translated Hangul, Koreanized loanwords, and raw English/Latin notation. Therefore Japanese can usually lead with a local term and add English secondarily, while Korean needs selective bilingual first use for more technical controls. Neither locale supports treating raw English as the universal primary display.

### Traditional Chinese (zh-Hant)

Traditional Chinese was independently checked against genuine Traditional-Chinese sources. The profile uses native Traditional-Chinese terms first, retains internationally recognizable glyphs and abbreviations secondarily, and preserves regional alternatives rather than converting Simplified-Chinese text mechanically.

Strong observations include 太陽, 月亮, 本命盤, 上升點, 中天 or 天頂, 宮頭, 整宮制, 普拉西德制, 合相, 對分相, 四分相, 三分相, 六分相, 容許度, 入相, and 出相. Regional variation is material for 中天/天頂, 對分相/對沖, 四分相/刑相, 三分相/拱相, and the exact wording of aspect phases. Those alternatives remain aliases or review candidates, not approved production variants.

Raw English should not be the primary display language for core zh-Hant concepts. The English token orb may remain a searchable alias or technical secondary notation only.

## Evidence highlights

### ASC and MC

ASC and MC are the most stable cross-locale abbreviations in the bounded sample. Spanish exposes Ascendente and Medio Cielo, French exposes Ascendant and Milieu du Ciel, German exposes Aszendent and Medium Coeli/Himmelsmitte, Japanese exposes アセンダント and MC with ミッドヘブン or メディウム・コエリ, Korean exposes 상승점 and 중천점 alongside 어센던트 and 미드헤븐, and Traditional Chinese exposes 上升點 plus 中天 or 天頂. The matrix therefore treats ASC and MC as secondary international notation, while leaving the locale-first wording to review where alternatives are material.

Evidence result: **ASC is convergent in all six locales. MC is usable in all six, but the preferred locale-first label remains a review decision for ja, ko, and zh-Hant.**

### Orb, applying, exact, and separating

Orb is comparatively stable: Spanish Orbe, French Orbe, German Orbis, Japanese オーブ, Korean 오브 or 오비스, and Traditional Chinese 容許度 or 容許範圍. Applying, exact, and separating are not equally stable. French Astrotheme directly exposes appliquant, exact, and séparant language; Spanish and German expose related technical forms; Japanese, Korean, and Traditional-Chinese sources expose useful candidates but with more mixed professional usage, English or transliterated alternatives, and less consistent first-use grammar.

Evidence result: **Orb has strong local candidates in every locale. Applying and separating remain review-sensitive, especially ja, ko, and zh-Hant. Exact is usable but still needs a consistent state-versus-qualifier decision.**

### House systems

Placidus behaves as a retained proper name in all six profiles. Whole Sign is observed as Signos enteros, Signes entiers, Ganzzeichenhäuser, ホールサイン, 홀사인 하우스, and 整宮制. Equal House is observed as casas iguales, Maisons égales, Äquale Häuser, イコールハウス, 이퀄 하우스, and 等宮制. The English system names and glyph-like short forms are retained as secondary discovery aids only.

Evidence result: **Placidus can use PROPER_NAME_RETAINED. Whole Sign and Equal House have usable candidates but require a final product register decision in each locale.**

### Japanese and Korean evidence boundary

Japanese and Korean have enough evidence for the bodies, signs, core aspects, retrograde, natal chart, angles, and house labels to form conservative proposals. Their technical phase terminology is not equally corroborated. The matrix deliberately does not turn the most visible English or loanword form into an approved production term.

### Traditional-Chinese evidence boundary

The zh-Hant matrix was independently verified from genuine Traditional-Chinese sources. The source set includes zh-TW pages, Traditional-Chinese Hong Kong/Taiwan material, and Traditional-Chinese Wikipedia as a secondary reference. A Simplified-Chinese-only page that was encountered during research was excluded from the evidence base. No automatic character conversion, machine translation, or zh-Hans-to-zh-Hant substitution was used.

## Review queue

The following queue is intentionally short and ordered by terminology risk rather than by implementation convenience.

The matrix marks **24 locale-concept records** as REVIEW_REQUIRED. These are review flags, not approvals; the same semantic concept can appear in more than one locale.

1. **Aspect phase family across ja, ko, and zh-Hant:** decide whether the production display leads with a local descriptive term, a conventional loanword, or a bilingual state label for applying and separating; decide the exact-state wording alongside them.
2. **Japanese MC:** select ミッドヘブン or メディウム・コエリ as the locale-first label, with MC retained as notation.
3. **Korean MC and aspect register:** decide between Korean descriptive terms and loanword aliases for 중천점 and the five major aspects.
4. **Traditional-Chinese angles and aspect variants:** choose between 中天 and 天頂, and between paired variants such as 對分相/對沖, 四分相/刑相, and 三分相/拱相 without erasing regional aliases.
5. **Whole Sign and Equal House in all locales:** select product-length labels and first-use explanations; preserve Placidus as a proper name.
6. **House number typography:** choose one Arabic, Roman, or locale-native display convention for house rows while retaining aliases for search.
7. **Spanish phase grammar and Whole Sign:** confirm adjective agreement and whether casas de signos enteros or a shorter label leads.
8. **French and German phase inflection:** confirm the noun phrase that controls appliquant/appliquante, séparant/séparante, applikativ, and separativ.
9. **Traditional-Chinese cusp and system register:** decide whether 宮頭 and the system transliterations are sufficiently clear for a general audience.

## Publication and scope boundary

This benchmark intentionally stops before production localization. The owner must resolve the review queue before any candidate can be promoted into the production glossary. Until then:

- Every non-English matrix candidate remains PROPOSED_NOT_APPROVED.
- English remains the canonical baseline.
- No production glossary variants were added.
- No semantic IDs or glossary families were changed.
- No runtime source, tests, server source, engine source, configuration, migration, package file, or UI source was changed.
- No P1 or P2 concept was benchmarked.
- No long article, interpretation, chart reading, or other copyrighted prose was copied. Only short terminology observations, notation, aliases, and source URLs are recorded.

This document is a research artifact for a later owner decision. It is not a release artifact and must not be consumed by runtime code without a separate approval task.
